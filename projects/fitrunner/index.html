FitRunner

Fitness runner you play with your body: a PC webcam or a phone camera tracks you as you run, jump, crouch, and steer.

Download the playtest builds (Google Drive) Version 0.2.0 for Windows, Android, and iOS. The Windows build is about 750 MB. Start with Readme.txt.

I am building FitRunner. You jog in place, jump, crouch, and step sideways in front of a camera, and the runner on screen does the same. Pose tracking runs with MediaPipe on the device that has the camera, and only movement data and commands are sent to the game, never video.

Normal mode uses jogging in place to set the runner's pace, and pumping your arms makes it faster. Easy mode runs automatically, so the player only has to dodge. The game has a 991 m authored forest course and an endless mode built from pooled track segments. A voiced tutorial teaches the moves on the first run, and coins collected along the way unlock characters.

The hardest problems have been latency and accuracy. Sideways movement uses short prediction from timestamped pose samples and corrects itself as new samples arrive. I log play sessions as pose data, more than 50 recordings so far, and tune the jump, pace, and steering detectors by replaying selected runs through them. One example: a sideways step used to stop the runner in 38 of 185 steps, and after a fix, replayed on the same recordings, 12 still do.

Version 0.2.0 is out for playtesting. The Windows app plays with the PC's own camera, or with a phone as the camera. The phone finds the PC on the local network, pairs by code or QR, and reconnects if the connection drops. There are also early builds of the whole game for Android and iOS phones and tablets.

I direct AI coding agents for much of the code. The architecture and design decisions, device testing, and recordings are mine, and so are the course route, obstacles, and bridges, built on terrain and scenery I migrated into Unity from the Dreamscape Fab pack. Characters and animations come from free packs. The project has over 2,500 automated tests, and the motion code is also checked by replays of recorded sessions.

The video shows a run of the forest course, recorded just before version 0.2.0, with a phone as the camera and the game on a budget laptop (Ryzen 5, RTX 2050).
